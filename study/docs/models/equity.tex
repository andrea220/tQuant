\section{Equity}\label{sec:mp-equity}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Underlying, forward and dividends}\label{sec:mp-forward}
An equity is described by the spot $S_0$, a repo (or borrow) rate $q_r$, dividends (either a cash schedule $D_i$ with
ex-dates $t_i$ and payment dates $p_i$, or a continuous yield $q$), the discount curve $P(0,T)$ of its currency and a
Black vol surface.
The forward (\code{Equity.forward}, the single implementation used by every pricer and model):
\begin{align*}
  \text{cash:}\quad & F(T) = \frac{\big(S_0 - \mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{div}}(T)\big)\,e^{-q_r T}}{P(0,T)},\qquad
    \mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{div}}(T) = \sum_{t_i \le T} D_i\,P(0,p_i); \\
  \text{yield:}\quad & F(T) = \frac{S_0\,e^{-(q + q_r)T}}{P(0,T)}.
\end{align*}
A dividend enters the horizon by its ex-date (the stock drops then) and is discounted from its payment date (the
cash; the ex-date when no payment date is given). With cash dividends $F$ drops by about $D_i$ at each ex-date and
grows at $r - q_r$ in between. The implied dividend
yield $q_{\mathrm{eff}}(T) = -\ln\big(1 - \mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{div}}(T)/S_0\big)/T$ is the flat yield with the same forward:
it steps up at each ex-date and decays as $1/T$ in between.

\emph{Sensitivities.} $\partial F/\partial S_0 = e^{-q_r T}/P(0,T)$ (cash dividends do not move with the spot),
$\partial F/\partial q_r = -T F$, $\partial F/\partial D_i = -P(0,p_i)\,e^{-q_r T}/P(0,T)$ for $t_i \le T$ and $0$ after;
on the curve, the pillars around $T$ (through $P(0,T)$) and around each payment date of a dividend with $t_i \le T$
(through $\mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{div}}$).

\emph{ATM.} The at-the-money vol of a maturity is the vol at $K = F(T)$, not at $S_0$: with dividends the forward is
well below the spot (TICKER1: $F/S_0 = 0.86$ at 5Y), and on a skewed smile the two differ by up to a vol point.

\emph{Forward ratio.} The Monte Carlo models simulate $X_t = S_t/F(t)$, a martingale with $X_0 = 1$, and return
$S_t = F(t)\,X_t$: the market forward is matched at every date by construction, and cash dividends act as proportional
drops in the dynamics. \nb{equity\_underlying.ipynb}

\paragraph{Dividend models in Black--Scholes.} \nb{dividends.ipynb}
With cash dividends $D_i$ at ex-dates $t_i \le T$, $\mathrm{PV} = \sum_i D_i\,P(0,p_i)$, repo $q_r$, $r = -\ln P(0,T)/T$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{discrete (escrowed)}: lognormal $S^* = S - \mathrm{PV}$, $F = (S_0 - \mathrm{PV})\,e^{(r - q_r)T}$;
  \item \emph{continuous}: yield $q_{\mathrm{eff}} = -\ln(1 - \mathrm{PV}/S_0)/T$ on the whole $S$,
        $F = S_0\,e^{(r - q_{\mathrm{eff}} - q_r)T}$: the same forward.
\end{itemize}
A European price depends only on $(F, \sigma, T, P(0,T))$: identical in both. What differs:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Delta.} Discrete: the cash amounts are fixed (sticky cash), $\partial F/\partial S = e^{(r-q_r)T}$.
        Continuous with $q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ held (sticky yield): the dividends scale with $S$, so
        $\Delta_{\mathrm{cont}} = \Delta_{\mathrm{disc}}\,(S_0 - \mathrm{PV})/S_0$.
  \item \emph{American options} (Bjerksund--Stensland 1993, carry $b = r - q$): discrete, $b = r - q_r \ge r - q_r$, so
        the call is (almost) never exercised early and the put has the larger premium; continuous with
        $q_{\mathrm{eff}} > r$, the call has an early-exercise premium and the put almost none. The approximation is
        within about 1\% of a binomial tree (a lower bound; it keeps less of a small premium far out of the money).
  \item \emph{Dynamics.} The escrowed model applies $\sigma$ to $S - \mathrm{PV}$, the real stock drops by $D_i$ at
        $t_i$ with $\sigma$ on the whole $S$: same forward, more variance. Matching vol of $S^*$ period by period,
        $\sigma^{*2}\,T \approx \sigma^2 \sum_k \big(S_0/(S_0 - \mathrm{PV}_k)\big)^2 \Delta t_k$ ($\mathrm{PV}_k$: dividends still
        to come in period $k$); the flat $\sigma\,S_0/(S_0 - \mathrm{PV})$ ignores the timing and overshoots
        (TICKER1 1Y: MC 24.46, time-weighted 24.40, flat 25.16).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implied volatility surface}\label{sec:mp-vol-surface}
Market quotes are Black implied vols $\sigma_{ij}$ on maturities $T_i$ (years) $\times$ absolute strikes $K_j$. The
surface used by the pricers (\code{VolatilitySurface.volatility(K, T)}) is bilinear in $(K, T)$ on the vols and flat
outside the grid: a vol between nodes depends on the 4 surrounding nodes only, so the vega of a price is bucketed
on the nodes (gradient w.r.t.\ the matrix). The interpolation is not arbitrage-free by construction (linear in vol,
not in total variance); the local vol model builds its own smooth representation in total variance and forward
moneyness (\S\ref{sec:mp-lv}). In the synthetic data the smiles come from an SSVI parametrisation (arbitrage-free on
the nodes): steep negative skew at short maturities, flattening with $T$.
\nb{market\_handles.ipynb}

\subsection{Black--Scholes}\label{sec:mp-bs}
European prices on the forward, dividend models, American (Bjerksund--Stensland), greeks by AAD.
\nb{black\_scholes.ipynb}

\emph{European.} On the forward of \S\ref{sec:mp-forward} and the surface vol at $(K, T)$, $\phi = \pm 1$:
\[
  V = P(0,T)\,\phi\,\big(F N(\phi d_1) - K N(\phi d_2)\big),\qquad
  d_{1,2} = \frac{\ln(F/K) \pm \tfrac12 \sigma^2 T}{\sigma\sqrt T},\qquad r = -\ln P(0,T)/T.
\]
Put--call parity $C - P = P(0,T)(F - K)$ holds to machine precision, hence $\Delta_C - \Delta_P = P(0,T)\,\partial F/\partial S$
($= e^{-q_r T}$ with cash dividends), $\Gamma_C = \Gamma_P$, $\mathcal{V}_C = \mathcal{V}_P$.

\emph{Greeks} by AAD on the market variables, one tape: delta and gamma (second tape around the first) on the spot;
vega on the surface nodes (bucketed: the bilinear weights of the 4 nodes around $(K,T)$; parallel vega = sum); rho
on the curve pillars; theta on the time to maturity, the only pricer-local variable ($r$ and the dividend inputs held,
so it is the pure time decay of the formula). Desk units: vega per vol point, theta per day, rho per bp.

\emph{American} (Bjerksund--Stensland 1993): a flat exercise boundary $I$ (trigger price) for the call with carry
$b = r - q$, closed form in terms of $\phi(S, T, \gamma, H, I)$ functions; puts by the symmetry
$P(S, K, r, b) = C(K, S, r - b, -b)$; immediate exercise above $I$; European value when $b \ge r$ (calls are not
exercised early without dividends). Accuracy: a lower bound within about 1\% of a binomial tree. The early-exercise
premium follows the carry (escrowed dividends: puts only; equivalent yield above $r$: calls only), grows with the
maturity and jumps at each ex-date entering the option life; below the boundary the American put is worth $K - S$
and its delta is $-1$.

\subsection{Delta hedging and gamma trading}\label{sec:mp-hedging}
Long a vanilla bought at the implied vol $\sigma_i$, short $\Delta$ shares (Black--Scholes delta at $\sigma_i$), rebalanced at
each step, cash at $r$, carry $q$ paid on the short stock. Over a step the hedged position earns
\[
  \tfrac12\,\Gamma S^2\Big(\big(\tfrac{\Delta S}{S}\big)^2 - \sigma_i^2\,\Delta t\Big)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \text{P\&L}_T \approx \tfrac12 \int_0^T \Gamma_t S_t^2\,\big(\sigma_r^2 - \sigma_i^2\big)\,\dd t,
\]
the gamma gain on the realised moves minus the theta paid at the implied vol. Consequences (TICKER1 1Y ATM call,
$\sigma_i = 30\%$, 4000 GBM paths):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Realised = implied}: the hedge replicates the option, mean P\&L $\approx 0$; the residual is the discrete
        rebalancing error, std $\propto 1/\sqrt n$ for $n$ rebalances (std $\times \sqrt n \approx 23$ from 12 to 1000).
  \item \emph{Long gamma = long realised vol}: mean P\&L $\approx \mathcal{V}\,(\sigma_r - \sigma_i)$ ($\pm 4.5$ for $\pm 5$ vol points,
        vega 0.90 per point); short gamma the opposite. A delta-hedged option is a bet on realised against implied vol.
  \item \emph{Path dependence}: the weight $\Gamma S^2$ makes vol realised near the strike count most; the same realised
        vol gives different P\&L on different paths.
\end{itemize}
\nb{vanilla\_options.ipynb}

\subsection{Digital options}\label{sec:mp-digital}
European digitals on $\phi(S_T - K) > 0$ ($\phi = \pm 1$), paid at $T$: cash-or-nothing (amount $A$) and asset-or-nothing
($S_T$). Under Black--Scholes, with the vol at the strike:
\[
  D^{\mathrm{cash}} = A\,P(0,T)\,N(\phi d_2),\qquad D^{\mathrm{asset}} = P(0,T)\,F\,N(\phi d_1);
\]
$N(\phi d_2)$ is the risk-neutral probability of finishing in the money. Model-free parities: cash call + cash put
$= A\,P(0,T)$, asset call + asset put $= P(0,T)\,F$, asset-or-nothing $- K\times$ unit cash-or-nothing $=$ vanilla.

\emph{Replication and skew.} The digital call is the limit of a call spread, $D(K) = -\partial C/\partial K$. With a smile
$\sigma(K)$ the vanilla price moves with the strike also through its vol:
\[
  -\frac{\partial C}{\partial K} = P(0,T)\,N(d_2) - \mathcal{V}\,\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial K},
\]
so a negative skew makes the market digital call dearer than the Black--Scholes one (TICKER1 1Y ATM: 0.431 vs 0.500)
and the digital put cheaper. A digital is priced off the \emph{slope} of the smile: with vols linear between strike
nodes the slope is piecewise constant and the call-spread digital jumps at the nodes; a model with a smooth smile
(local vol, spline in total variance) gives a slightly different value (0.493). A finite spread $h$ brackets the digital,
$\frac{C(K) - C(K+h)}{h} \le D(K) \le \frac{C(K-h) - C(K)}{h}$, at the cost of $1/h$ vanillas per unit of payoff.

\emph{Greeks} (cash-or-nothing call, unit amount):
$\Delta = P(0,T)\,n(d_2)/(S\sigma\sqrt T)$, a bump centred on the strike that sharpens as $T \to 0$ (TICKER1: peak 0.017 at
1M, 0.005 at 1Y); $\Gamma = -P(0,T)\,n(d_2)\,d_1/(S^2\sigma^2 T)$ and $\mathcal{V} = -P(0,T)\,n(d_2)\,d_1/\sigma$ change sign at
$d_1 = 0$, close to the strike: long gamma and long vega out of the money, short both in the money (the vega flip).
\emph{Hedging}: not with the underlying alone near expiry (the delta spike and the gamma flip at the strike: pin
risk), but statically with a call spread of width $h$, which carries the digital's delta, gamma and vega (flip and skew
term included) with greeks bounded by $1/h$; the seller buys the over-replicating spread $[K - h, K]$ (the difference is a
reserve), $h$ set by the vanilla liquidity and the gamma the book can hold; only the spread-vs-digital residual is
hedged dynamically, on the smile.

\emph{Monte Carlo.} The payoff is an indicator: its pathwise derivative is zero almost everywhere, so AAD gives no
delta or vega. A sigmoid of width $\varepsilon K$ replaces it (\code{digital\_smoothing}): smooth payoff, AAD greeks, a
price bias of second order in the width (0.002 at $\varepsilon = 1\%$ on a 0.43 digital).
\nb{digital\_options.ipynb}

\subsection{Monte Carlo framework}\label{sec:mp-mc}
Time grid, stateless draws and common random numbers, pathwise AAD greeks, martingale correction.
\nb{monte\_carlo\_gbm.ipynb}

The price is the mean of $N$ discounted payoffs on simulated paths, with standard error
$\mathrm{std}(\text{payoff})/\sqrt N$: the error falls as $1/\sqrt N$ whatever the dimension (TICKER1 1Y ATM call, 256k paths:
s.e.\ 0.09 on 27.06). A model simulates on a time grid ending at the payoff dates; the GBM (escrowed spot, carry = repo,
vol at $(K,T)$, the same model as Black--Scholes discrete) is simulated exactly, so one step suffices for a European
payoff; models with state-dependent vol (local vol, Heston) need small steps.

\emph{Random numbers.} Draws are stateless and seeded: the same seed gives the same paths. A bump repriced with the
same seed uses common random numbers, so the noise of the two prices cancels in the difference; with independent
seeds the finite-difference greek is dominated by noise ($\propto 1/h$: delta 1.41 instead of 0.56 with a 0.1 bump).

\emph{Variance reduction} (not in the pricers yet): antithetic variates ($Z$ and $-Z$; s.e.\ 0.151 $\to$ 0.124 at equal
cost) and the control variate on $S_T$, whose mean is the forward ($\hat C = \overline{C} - \beta(\overline{S_T} - F)$; s.e.\
$\to$ 0.064).

\emph{Greeks by AAD.} The tape over the simulation gives the pathwise derivative of the mean payoff w.r.t.\ every
market variable the model was built from: delta, vega per node, rho per pillar, dividend sensitivities, in one
backward pass (MC vs Black--Scholes: delta 0.5591 / 0.5593, rho 103.29 / 103.24). Pathwise derivatives need a
Lipschitz payoff (calls, puts); for digitals and barriers the payoff is smoothed (\S\ref{sec:mp-digital}).
