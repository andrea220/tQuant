\section{Models}\label{sec:mp-models}
The simulation models. Each one is described by its dynamics, how it is calibrated to the market, its closed-form
prices when it has them, the simulation scheme and how its sensitivities are defined.

\paragraph{Common conventions.} The equity models are written on the \emph{forward ratio}
\[
  X_t = \frac{S_t}{F(t)},\qquad X_0 = 1,\qquad S_t = F(t)\,X_t ,
\]
with $F(t)$ the market forward of \S\ref{sec:mp-forward}. Under the risk-neutral measure $X$ is a martingale, so
every model reproduces the market forward at every date by construction: rates, repo and dividends enter only through
$F(t)$, and a cash dividend is a drop of $S$ proportional to its level. In Monte Carlo the simulated $X_t$ can optionally
be rescaled to sample mean one at each date, which removes the noise on the forward.

\paragraph{Sensitivities.} A sensitivity is the derivative of the price w.r.t.\ one market quote, all the other quotes
held, \emph{with the model recalibrated}: it is the limit of ``bump the quote, recalibrate, reprice with the same random
numbers''. The quotes are the spot, the repo, the cash dividend amounts, the zero rates at the curve pillars and the
implied vol at each node $(T_i, K_j)$ of the surface. Holding the other quotes fixes the scenario convention: when the spot
moves, the implied vols stay at their \emph{absolute} strikes (sticky strike), the dividends stay as cash amounts and the
curves as zero rates (another convention, e.g.\ vols fixed in moneyness, gives another delta). Through a calibration by
optimisation the derivative of the parameters comes from the implicit function theorem at the optimum; through an
explicit construction (local vol) from the chain rule. In Monte Carlo the derivative is taken path by path (pathwise
estimator, \citealp{giles2006,glasserman2003}), all quotes at once by algorithmic differentiation \citep{savine2018}; it equals
the bump derivative of the same estimator, and needs a payoff continuous in the path (indicators are smoothed). Units:
delta per unit of spot, vega per vol point and per node, rho per basis point and per pillar.

\subsection{Geometric Brownian motion}\label{sec:mp-gbm}
\paragraph{Dynamics.} $\dd S_t = \mu S_t\,\dd t + \sigma S_t\,\dd W_t$, with constant drift and vol: the Black--Scholes model
\citep{black1973,merton1973}. $\ln S_T$ is Gaussian, so a European option has the Black--Scholes price
(\S\ref{sec:mp-vanilla}).

\paragraph{Calibration.} One model per option $(K, T)$, nothing to optimise:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item $\sigma$ is the implied vol of the surface at $(K, T)$ (bilinear between the four surrounding nodes);
  \item $r = -\ln P(0,T)/T$, the zero rate to the expiry;
  \item with cash dividends the simulated process is the escrowed spot $S^* = S_0 - \mathrm{PV}_{\mathrm{div}}(T)$ with drift $\mu = r - q_r$;
        with a dividend yield it is the spot with $\mu = r - q - q_r$.
\end{enumerate}
The model reprices the option it is built for and nothing else: it is the Monte Carlo counterpart of the Black--Scholes
formula, not a model of the surface. With cash dividends the escrowed path is the spot only at $T$, so the model is not
fit for products observed before the expiry.

\paragraph{Simulation.} Exact: $S_{t+\Delta} = S_t\,\exp\big((\mu - \tfrac12\sigma^2)\Delta + \sigma\sqrt\Delta\,Z\big)$, $Z \sim N(0,1)$; one step is
enough for a European payoff.

\paragraph{Sensitivities.} The chain is short,
\[
  \text{quotes} \;\to\; \big(S^*,\ \mu,\ r,\ \sigma(K, T)\big) \;\to\; S_T \;\to\; \text{payoff},
\]
and the simulation is exact, so the Monte Carlo sensitivities are the Black--Scholes ones up to the Monte Carlo error:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Delta}: $\partial S^*/\partial S_0 = 1$ and the drift does not depend on the spot, so the delta of a call is
        $e^{-q_r T} N(d_1)$, the Black--Scholes delta of the discrete-dividend model ($e^{-(q + q_r)T} N(d_1)$ with a yield); the vol
        stays at $(K, T)$ (sticky strike).
  \item \emph{Vega}: $\sigma$ is the bilinear interpolation of the four nodes around $(K, T)$, so the vega splits on these four
        nodes with the interpolation weights, and sums to the Black--Scholes vega.
  \item \emph{Rates}: $r$ enters the drift and the discounting through $P(0,T)$ only (the pillars around $T$), and the
        present value of the dividends through their payment dates.
  \item \emph{Dividends}: $\partial S^*/\partial D_i = -P(0,p_i)$ for every ex-date before $T$, so $\partial V/\partial D_i = -\Delta\,P(0, p_i)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Displaced diffusion}\label{sec:mp-dd}
\paragraph{Dynamics.} A mixture of lognormal and normal dynamics \citep{rubinstein1983}:
\[
  \dd X_t = \sigma(t)\,\big(\beta(t)\,X_t + 1 - \beta(t)\big)\,\dd W_t ,
\]
with $\beta$ and $\sigma$ piecewise constant between the surface maturities. The local vol of $X$ is
$\sigma(t)\,\big(\beta + (1 - \beta)/X\big)$: $\beta = 1$ is lognormal (flat smile), $\beta \to 0$ normal, with a vol that rises as $X$ falls
(negative skew). The skew per unit log-moneyness ranges from 0 to about $-\sigma/2$, so the model cannot produce a
steeper equity skew, and it has no smile curvature. With constant parameters $Y = \beta X + 1 - \beta$ is lognormal with vol
$\beta\sigma$, which gives calls on $X$ in closed form:
\[
  \E\big[(X_T - k)^+\big] = \frac{\mathrm{Black}\big(1,\ \beta k + 1 - \beta,\ \beta\sigma,\ T\big)}{\beta}
  \qquad\big(1 - k \text{ if } \beta k + 1 - \beta \le 0\big),
\]
puts by parity.

\paragraph{Calibration.} Two stages:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Slices}: for each maturity $T_i$, the constant parameters $\theta_i = (\beta_{T_i}, \sigma_{T_i})$ that best fit the smile of $T_i$:
        least squares on the market calls per unit forward $c_{ij}$ at the strike nodes $k_{ij} = K_j / F(T_i)$,
        \[
          \min_{\theta_i}\ L_i = \tfrac12 \sum_j \big(c^{\mathrm{DD}}(k_{ij}, T_i;\ \theta_i) - c_{ij}\big)^2 ,\qquad \beta \in [0.02, 1.5];
        \]
        two parameters fit the level and the skew of the slice, not its curvature;
  \item \emph{Forward pieces}: the time-dependent parameters on $[T_{i-1}, T_i)$ that reproduce the slices. The variance adds
        up, $\sigma_i^2 (T_i - T_{i-1}) = \sigma_{T_i}^2 T_i - \sigma_{T_{i-1}}^2 T_{i-1}$; the effective $\beta$ of a maturity is an average of
        the $\beta(t)$ before it, weighted towards the later times \citep{piterbarg2005,andersen2010},
        \[
          \beta_{T} = \frac{\sum_j \beta_j W_j}{\sum_j W_j},\qquad W_j = \int_{\text{piece } j} \sigma^2(t)\,v(t)\,\dd t,\quad v(t) = \int_0^t \sigma^2(s)\,\dd s ,
        \]
        solved maturity by maturity for the $\beta_j$ (a linear equation each). Slices whose skews are not consistent in
        time give a forward $\beta$ out of its range.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Simulation.} Exact on each piece (shifted lognormal step); a step across pieces uses the variance-weighted
$\beta$ and the summed variance.

\paragraph{Sensitivities.} The chain goes through an optimisation:
\[
  \text{quotes} \;\to\; \big(F(T_i),\ k_{ij},\ c_{ij}\big) \;\to\; \theta_i = (\beta_{T_i}, \sigma_{T_i}) \;\to\; (\beta_j, \sigma_j) \;\to\; X_t \;\to\; S_t = F(t)\,X_t \;\to\; \text{payoff}.
\]
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Through the slice fit} (implicit function theorem, \citealp{henrard2011}). At the optimum the gradient of $L_i$ is
        zero, $\nabla_\theta L_i(\theta_i^*, m) = 0$, for every value of the market inputs $m$; differentiating this identity,
        \[
          \frac{\partial\theta_i^*}{\partial m} = -H_i^{-1}\,\frac{\partial^2 L_i}{\partial\theta\,\partial m},\qquad H_i = \nabla^2_\theta L_i(\theta_i^*, m),
        \]
        the derivative of a re-optimisation without re-optimising (a parameter on a bound stays fixed). The forward
        pieces are explicit functions of the slices (chain rule).
  \item \emph{Vega per node}: a node $(T_i, K_j)$ changes only $c_{ij}$, hence only the slice $\theta_i$ (each slice is fitted
        alone), then the forward pieces from $T_i$ on. A vanilla at $T_i$ gets vega on all the strikes of its slice, in
        proportion to how much each node pulls the two parameters, not only on the node at its strike as in local vol.
  \item \emph{Delta}: a higher spot raises the forwards, so the paths scale and, with the vols held at absolute strikes,
        the nodes move in moneyness $k_{ij}$ and the slices are refitted. A vanilla is repriced only up to the fit
        residual, so its delta is the Black--Scholes sticky-strike one up to the slope of that residual.
  \item \emph{Rates, repo, dividends}: through the forwards, as the delta without the move of the spot.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Local volatility}\label{sec:mp-lv}
\paragraph{Dynamics.} The volatility is a deterministic function of time and spot \citep{dupire1994,derman1994}:
\[
  \frac{\dd S_t}{S_t} = \mu_t\,\dd t + \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(t, S_t)\,\dd W_t
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  \frac{\dd X_t}{X_t} = \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(t, y_t)\,\dd W_t,\quad y_t = \ln X_t .
\]
There is one source of randomness; the instantaneous vol of the stock is known once the spot is known. The function
$\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is not chosen: it is \emph{implied} by the market, as the only diffusion of this form whose distribution of
$S_T$ at every maturity is the one of the market option prices. Below, $k = K/F(T)$ and $c(k, T) = \E[(X_T - k)^+]$ is the
undiscounted call per unit of forward.

\paragraph{From option prices to local vol.} Three steps.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\Alph*)]
  \item \emph{Prices give the distributions} \citep{breeden1978}. Differentiating $c = \int_k^\infty (x - k)\,p_T(x)\,\dd x$ in the strike,
        \[
          \partial_k c = -\Q(X_T > k),\qquad \partial_{kk} c = p_T(k):
        \]
        a butterfly $c(k-h) - 2c(k) + c(k+h)$ divided by $h^2$ pays a unit-area triangle around $k$ and tends to the density.
        The calls of one maturity are the risk-neutral distribution at that maturity; no butterfly arbitrage $\Leftrightarrow$
        $c$ convex in $k$ $\Leftrightarrow$ $p_T \ge 0$.
  \item \emph{The distributions give the local vol} \citep{dupire1994}. The distributions alone do not fix how the stock moves
        between maturities; within the diffusions above there is exactly one that has them all. Itô's formula on the
        payoff $(X_T - k)^+$ (Tanaka: $f' = \mathbf 1\{x > k\}$, $f'' = \delta(x - k)$) and expectation: the linear part has zero
        mean ($X$ is a martingale) and only the kink at the strike contributes,
        \[
          \partial_T c = \tfrac12\,\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2(T, k)\,k^2\,p_T(k) = \tfrac12\,\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2(T, k)\,k^2\,\partial_{kk} c
          \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
          \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2(T, k) = \frac{\partial_T c}{\tfrac12\,k^2\,\partial_{kk} c} .
        \]
        It is the Black--Scholes theta--gamma relation read in strike: the calls grow with the maturity only through the
        paths at the strike, in proportion to the local variance and to the density there. Known prices determine the
        local variance: calibration is a computation, not an optimisation.
  \item \emph{Meaning} \citep{gyongy1986,derman1994}. For any model with a stochastic instantaneous vol $\sigma_t$ that reprices the
        same calls, $\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2(T, k) = \E[\sigma_T^2 \mid X_T = k]$: the local variance is the expected instantaneous variance
        on the paths through $(T, k)$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The formula in implied total variance.} With forward log-moneyness $y = \ln k$ and total implied variance
$w(y, T) = \sigma_{\mathrm{imp}}^2(y, T)\,T$, the market call is the Black--Scholes price $c = c_{\mathrm{BS}}(y, w(y, T))$, which depends
on $y$ and $w$ only. In $y$ the denominator of Dupire is $\tfrac12(\partial_{yy} c - \partial_y c)$. By the chain rule the numerator is
$\partial_T c = \partial_w c_{\mathrm{BS}}\,\partial_T w$, and the denominator, with the Black--Scholes identities, is $\partial_w c_{\mathrm{BS}}\; g$; the common factor
cancels \citep[eq.~1.10]{gatheral2006}:
\[
  \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2 = \frac{\partial_T w}{g},\qquad
  g = 1 - \frac{y}{w}\,\partial_y w + \frac14\Big(-\frac14 - \frac1w + \frac{y^2}{w^2}\Big)(\partial_y w)^2 + \frac12\,\partial_{yy} w .
\]
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Numerator} (calendar term): the variance the market adds per unit of time at fixed moneyness, the forward
        variance.
  \item \emph{Denominator} (butterfly term): $g$ is the density relative to the lognormal one with the same total variance,
        $p(y) = g(y)\,e^{-d_-^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi w}$ with $d_- = -y/\sqrt w - \sqrt w/2$. The skew term $-\frac yw\,\partial_y w$ is below $0$ on the downside
        of a negative skew: lower $g$, higher local vol there.
  \item \emph{No static arbitrage}, also the condition for a local vol to exist: $\partial_T w \ge 0$ at fixed $y$ (calendar spreads)
        and $g > 0$ (butterflies).
  \item \emph{No skew}: $g = 1$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2 = \partial_T w$; a flat surface gives a flat local vol.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Calibration.} There is no parameter to fit: calibrating means building, from the implied vol nodes $\sigma_{ij}$
(maturities $T_i$, strikes $K_j$), a surface $w(y, T)$ defined everywhere that passes through the nodes, has continuous
$\partial_y w$, $\partial_{yy} w$ in closed form, keeps $\partial_T w \ge 0$ and has wings without arbitrage; then applying the formula. The fit is
exact at the nodes by construction. The steps:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Coordinates.} Each node goes to $y_{ij} = \ln(K_j / F(T_i))$ and $w_{ij} = \sigma_{ij}^2 T_i$ (the same strike has a different $y$ at
        each maturity, since the forward moves).
  \item \emph{Smile per maturity.} $w(\cdot, T_i)$ is a natural cubic spline in $y$ through the nodes (the least-bending curve with
        continuous curvature), so $\partial_y w$ and $\partial_{yy} w$ are analytic: no finite differences of option prices, which are tiny
        at short maturities and in the wings, and no slope jumps (linear interpolation in vol puts a spike in the
        density at every node). Outside the nodes it continues linearly with the slope capped at $1.9$, below the
        bound $2$ of \citet{lee2004} on the wings of the total variance.
  \item \emph{Between maturities.} At fixed $y$, $w$ is linear in $T$ (the same weights for $\partial_y w$, $\partial_{yy} w$), so $\partial_T w$ is the
        forward variance of each interval and is positive whenever the slices do not cross; before the first maturity
        $w$ is linear from $0$ (flat implied vol), after the last the implied vol is flat. The local vol therefore jumps
        at each quoted maturity.
  \item \emph{The formula on a grid.} $\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}$ on a grid of time step $1/96$ year (plus the maturities) and 300 points of
        $y \in [\ln 0.05, \ln 5]$, with $g$ floored at $10^{-3}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}$ capped at 200\%; the simulation reads it by bilinear
        interpolation at $y = \ln(S_t/F(t))$.
  \item \emph{Arbitrage check.} The cells within the quoted strikes where $g < 10^{-3}$ or $\partial_T w \le 0$ are reported as static
        arbitrage: there no local vol can reprice the surface.
\end{enumerate}
Between the nodes this representation (spline in total variance) is not the bilinear interpolation in vol used by the
closed-form pricers of \S\ref{sec:mp-vol-surface}: the two agree at the nodes only.

\paragraph{How implied and local vol relate.} They are different objects: the implied vol is the constant vol that
reprices one option, the local vol the instantaneous vol at $(t, S)$. The implied variance of $(K, T)$ is approximately
the time average of the local variance over the paths that end at the strike \citep[eqs.~3.8--3.11]{gatheral2006},
concentrated around the straight line from the spot to the strike in log-moneyness:
\[
  \sigma_{\mathrm{imp}}^2(K, T) \approx \frac1T\int_0^T \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2\big(t, \tfrac tT\,y_K\big)\,\dd t .
\]
Consequences:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Rule of 2} \citep{derman1996}: with $\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}} \approx a + b\,y$ near the money and a short maturity,
        $\sigma_{\mathrm{imp}}(y) \approx \frac1y\int_0^y (a + b z)\,\dd z = a + \frac b2\,y$: the implied skew is about half the local skew. At longer
        maturities the average also runs over the term structure and the ratio moves away from 2.
  \item A steep short-dated implied wing requires a much higher local vol there; with a convex smile the local vol at
        the money is below the implied vol (the paths also visit the higher-vol wings).
  \item The fit is checked by repricing: options simulated under the local vol, inverted with Black, give back the
        market implied vols.
  \item The local skew decays with the maturity, so the \emph{forward} smile of the model (the smile of $S_{T_2}/S_{T_1}$) is
        flatter than today's smile of the same length \citep[ch.~3 and 8]{gatheral2006}; see also \citet{derman2016}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Simulation.} Log-Euler on $\ln X$ with the local vol at the start of each step:
\[
  \ln X_{t+\Delta} = \ln X_t - \tfrac12\,\sigma^2\Delta + \sigma\sqrt\Delta\,Z,\qquad \sigma = \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(t, \ln X_t),
\]
$\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}$ bilinear on its grid. The scheme has a bias of first order in the step: on an equity surface, at the maturities
from 6M to 3Y, the repriced smile is about $0.2$ vol points high on average with 50 steps a year (at most $0.5$), and the bias
is gone with 200 steps a year; with $2\cdot10^4$ paths the Monte Carlo error is of a few tenths of a vol point.

\paragraph{Sensitivities.} The local vol is rebuilt from the quotes for every price, so each sensitivity runs through
the construction above (chain rule), then through the paths:
\begin{align*}
  \text{quotes} \;&\to\; \big(F(T_i),\ y_{ij},\ w_{ij}\big) \;\to\; \big(w,\ \partial_y w,\ \partial_{yy} w,\ \partial_T w\big)(y, T)\\
  &\to\; \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}(t, y) \;\to\; X_t \;\to\; S_t = F(t)\,X_t \;\to\; \text{payoff}.
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Delta.} A higher spot raises every forward ($\partial F/\partial S_0 = e^{-q_r T}/P(0,T)$ with cash dividends), so the paths
        scale with it; with the implied vols held at their absolute strikes, every node moves to a lower log-moneyness
        $y_{ij} = \ln(K_j/F(T_i))$, so the local vol seen by a path at a given spot changes too (the smile is re-read relative
        to the new forward). For a vanilla the two effects add up to a simple result: the model reprices every vanilla,
        so its price as a function of the spot is the Black--Scholes price at the fixed market vol $\sigma(K, T)$, and the delta
        is the Black--Scholes sticky-strike delta (with the same dividend model). For a path-dependent product the delta
        also contains the shift of the local vol along the paths, which a single Black--Scholes vol cannot represent.
  \item \emph{Vega per node.} A bump of the node $(T_i, K_j)$ raises $w$ at $T_i$ around $y_{ij}$ (through the spline, with small
        ripples on the neighbouring strikes) and changes $\partial_T w$ on the two adjacent time intervals: the local vol rises
        before $T_i$ and falls after it, around that log-moneyness. A vanilla on the node has the market vega on that node
        and almost nothing elsewhere (its price is the Black--Scholes price at that vol); a path-dependent product
        collects vega on all the nodes whose region $(t, y)$ its paths cross, with signs that can differ by maturity.
  \item \emph{Rates, repo, dividends.} They enter through the forwards (and the discounting of the payoff): the same
        chain as the delta, without the move of the spot.
\end{itemize}
For vanillas these sensitivities match the closed-form Black--Scholes sticky-strike ones up to the Monte Carlo and
discretisation errors, which is the check of the construction; the information the model adds is in the exotics.

\paragraph{Limits.} All from the assumption of a deterministic vol:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Flat forward smile}: products on future smiles (forward-start options, cliquets, barriers and digitals
        observed at future dates in autocallables) are mispriced.
  \item \emph{Smile dynamics}: after a spot move the model's smile moves opposite to the observed one \citep{hagan2002}, so the
        model's own hedge is poor; in practice the surface is recalibrated and the greeks follow a market convention
        (sticky strike).
  \item \emph{No vol factor}: no vol of vol and a perfect spot--vol dependence; models that reprice the same vanillas
        (Heston, stochastic local vol) give different exotic prices, which local vol cannot measure.
  \item \emph{Interpolation-dependent}: between the nodes, in the wings and beyond the quoted maturities the local vol is a
        choice of construction; exotics that live there inherit it.
  \item \emph{Noise}: the second derivative in strike amplifies quote errors and arbitrages; the local vol surface and its
        vegas are unstable from one calibration to the next, hence arbitrage-free parametric smiles in practice
        \citep[SVI,][]{gatheral2006}.
  \item \emph{Short-dated skew}: largely jump risk in the market; a diffusion reproduces it only with extreme local vols in
        the wings.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Heston}\label{sec:mp-heston}
\paragraph{Dynamics.} The variance is a mean-reverting square-root process correlated with the spot \citep{heston1993}:
\[
  \frac{\dd X_t}{X_t} = \sqrt{v_t}\,\dd W^1_t,\qquad
  \dd v_t = \kappa(\theta - v_t)\,\dd t + \xi\sqrt{v_t}\,\dd W^2_t,\qquad \dd\langle W^1, W^2\rangle_t = \rho\,\dd t ,
\]
five constant parameters: initial variance $v_0$, long-run variance $\theta$, mean reversion $\kappa$, vol of vol $\xi$, correlation $\rho$.
$\rho < 0$ gives the negative skew, $\xi$ the curvature, $\kappa$ how fast both fade with the maturity. The variance stays
positive when $2\kappa\theta \ge \xi^2$ (Feller); otherwise it touches zero.

\paragraph{Closed form.} The characteristic function of $\ln X_T$ is known; in the form that is continuous in $u$
\citep{albrecher2007}, with $b = \kappa - \rho\xi iu$, $d = \sqrt{b^2 + \xi^2(iu + u^2)}$, $g = (b-d)/(b+d)$,
\[
  \varphi(u) = \exp\Big(\frac{\kappa\theta}{\xi^2}\Big[(b-d)T - 2\ln\frac{1 - g e^{-dT}}{1-g}\Big]
  + \frac{b-d}{\xi^2}\,\frac{1 - e^{-dT}}{1 - g e^{-dT}}\,v_0\Big).
\]
European options by the COS method \citep{fang2008}: the density of $\ln X_T$ is expanded in a cosine series on a range of
$\pm 30$ standard deviations around its mean (1024 terms); puts are integrated, calls follow by parity.

\paragraph{Calibration.} The five parameters are fitted to the whole surface at once:
\[
  \min_{v_0, \kappa, \theta, \xi, \rho}\ \sum_{i,j} \left(\frac{C^{\mathrm{Heston}}(k_{ij}, T_i) - C^{\mathrm{mkt}}(k_{ij}, T_i)}{\mathcal V^{\mathrm{ATM}}_{T_i}}\right)^2 ,
\]
calls per unit forward at the strike nodes $k_{ij} = K_j/F(T_i)$, each maturity divided by its ATM Black vega so that the
residuals are close to implied vol errors and all maturities weigh alike. Bounds: $v_0, \theta \in [10^{-4}, 1]$,
$\kappa \in [0.01, 20]$, $\xi \in [0.01, 5]$, $\rho \in [-0.99, 0.99]$; start from the short ATM variance for $v_0$, the average ATM variance
for $\theta$, $\kappa = 1.5$, $\xi = 0.5$, $\rho = -0.6$. Five parameters cannot fit a steep short-dated smile: the errors are
largest at the first maturities and in the wings. The sensitivities of the parameters to the market quotes come from
the implicit function theorem at the optimum, as for the displaced diffusion.

\paragraph{Simulation.} The quadratic-exponential scheme of \citet{andersen2008}. Given $v_t$, the next variance has mean
$m = \theta + (v_t - \theta)e^{-\kappa\Delta}$ and variance
$s^2 = v_t\,\xi^2 e^{-\kappa\Delta}(1 - e^{-\kappa\Delta})/\kappa + \theta\,\xi^2 (1 - e^{-\kappa\Delta})^2/(2\kappa)$; with $\psi = s^2/m^2$, it is drawn as
$a(b + Z)^2$ when $\psi \le 1.5$ (moments matched) and from a mass at zero plus an exponential tail otherwise, which keeps it
non-negative without truncation. $\ln X$ is then stepped with the time integral of $v$ approximated by the average of
the two ends and the drift chosen so that $X$ stays an exact martingale. Two normals per step.

\subsection{Heston stochastic local volatility}\label{sec:mp-slv}
\paragraph{Dynamics.} The Heston variance multiplied by a leverage function of time and spot:
\[
  \frac{\dd X_t}{X_t} = L(t, X_t)\,\sqrt{v_t}\,\dd W^1_t ,
\]
$v$ the calibrated Heston variance with the vol of vol scaled by a mixing factor $\eta \in (0, 1]$ ($\eta\,\xi$). The stochastic
variance gives the smile dynamics (forward smile); the leverage corrects the marginals.

\paragraph{Calibration of the leverage.} By Gyöngy's theorem the model has the local vol marginals if its local variance
$L^2(t, x)\,\E[v_t \mid X_t = x]$ equals the market one, which gives
\[
  L^2(t, x) = \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2(t, x)}{\E[v_t \mid X_t = x]} ,
\]
$\sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}$ the local vol of \S\ref{sec:mp-lv} (\citealp{vanderstoep2014,oosterlee2019}). The conditional expectation depends on the
leverage itself, so it is estimated on the simulated paths while they are generated (particle method,
\citealp{guyon2012}): at each step
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item 100 nodes span the 0.1\%--99.9\% quantiles of $\ln X_t$ over the paths;
  \item $\E[v_t \mid \ln X_t]$ at the nodes is the regression of $v_t$ on $\ln X_t$ with tent (piecewise linear) kernels; nodes
        with few paths are shrunk to the overall mean of $v_t$, and the result is floored at $10^{-4}$;
  \item each path gets $L = \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}/\sqrt{\E[v \mid X]}$ at its own $\ln X_t$ (capped at 10), and the step is taken.
\end{enumerate}
Small $\eta$ gives back the local vol model, $\eta = 1$ the most stochastic vol the Heston calibration allows.

\paragraph{Simulation.} QE step for $v$, log step of $X$ with $L$ frozen over the step, martingale correction per path.

\paragraph{Sensitivities.} By convention the Heston parameters and the conditional expectation $\E[v \mid X]$ are held
fixed; the leverage moves with the market through the local vol and the forward.

\subsection{Gaussian short-rate models}\label{sec:mp-gsr}
\paragraph{Dynamics.} The short rate is a sum of Gaussian factors plus a deterministic shift:
\[
  r(t) = \sum_i x_i(t) + \varphi(t),\qquad \dd x_i = -\kappa_i\,x_i\,\dd t + \sigma_i(t)\,\dd W^i_t,\qquad x_i(0) = 0 ,
\]
with correlated Brownians; one factor is Hull--White \citep{hull1990} (G1++), two factors G2++ \citep[ch.~3--4]{brigo2006}.
The vols are constant or piecewise constant in time. The shift $\varphi$ is chosen so that the model reproduces the market
discount curve exactly, so only the vol parameters are calibrated. With $V(t,T) = \mathrm{Var}\big(\int_t^T \sum_i x_i\big)$ (exact
integrals over the vol pieces) the zero-coupon bonds are
\[
  P(t,T) = \frac{P(0,T)}{P(0,t)}\exp\Big(\tfrac12\big[V(t,T) - V(0,T) + V(0,t)\big] - \sum_i B_i(T-t)\,x_i(t)\Big),\quad
  B_i(\tau) = \frac{1 - e^{-\kappa_i\tau}}{\kappa_i}.
\]

\paragraph{Closed forms.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Caplets}: an option to exchange two zero-coupon bonds, whose log ratio is Gaussian (\citealp{margrabe1978});
        the index curve enters through a deterministic multiplicative basis to the discount curve.
  \item \emph{Swaptions}: either the Gaussian swap-rate approximation (the swap rate linearised in the factors around
        zero, normal vol from its variance), or exact: the swap as a portfolio of bonds under the forward measure of the
        expiry, by \citet{jamshidian1989} with one factor, or with two by \citet{brigo2006} (Gauss--Hermite on the first
        factor, closed form on the second).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Calibration.} Least squares on caplets and / or swaptions: ATM (or given strikes) caplets up to a term, from
the normal caplet vols, and swaptions from the cube. Each residual is the model minus market price divided by the
normal vega, close to a normal vol error. Parameters inside bounds; a parameter can be held fixed. Hull--White with a
piecewise constant $\sigma(t)$ on the caplet fixings matches the ATM caplets exactly, with the mean reversion held (caplets
alone do not determine it). With two factors, swaptions of several tenors are needed to pin the correlation, which
caplets leave undetermined.

\paragraph{Simulation.} Exact joint Gaussian transition of the factors and of their time integrals; the bank-account
discount is $D(0,t) = P(0,t)\,e^{-\int_0^t \sum_i x_i - V(0,t)/2}$. Swap exposures (mark-to-market, expected positive and negative
exposure, potential future exposure) are computed on these paths.
